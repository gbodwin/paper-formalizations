# The better calibrated weak-cut factor on the original wire

The calibration computes both dyadic roots and compares them with measured
binary arithmetic. It executes the corresponding edge solver in each
adaptive step. A tie uses the mass-based mode. Each cutoff, exact
penalty, checked price and update follows the reached strategy.

Lean proves that the original-weight deletion factor of the accepted law
is bounded by a fixed multiple of the minimum of the weight-dependent
and size-dependent bounds. The literal-tape execution includes both
adaptive failure and bounded rejection at selection. For n at least two
it has failure at most n to the negative requested confidence exponent.
For positive confidence the accepted outcomes define a probability law
on valid cuts.

The packed entry reads the original adjacency and rational weights,
preserves field padding and requires the third table to be empty. It
computes threshold demands from the weights. Lean proves the packed
entry agrees with the typed runner on its encoded data, has a sufficient
tape bound polynomial in the stored source width and requested confidence,
and has a polynomial for the recorded deterministic charges.

The native cases exercise nine real adaptive size-mode calls and the
mass-mode tie. They also exercise the padded two-vertex wire, extra
trailing data and rejection of a supplied demand table.
